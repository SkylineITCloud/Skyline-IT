The Challenge

Built around women’s choices

You deserve to work, study, travel and spend time with others on your own terms. Violence and harassment are never your fault. Our aim is to make reaching people you trust easier.

What matters

  • A button you can find by touch
  • Clear signs that your button press was noticed
  • Knowing when location or mobile signal is missing
  • Working with affordable phones and less mobile data
  • Sharing without unwanted tracking

What guides our design

  • You choose the people you trust
  • You decide who can see your location and can stop future sharing
  • No public location pages or hidden tracking
  • Know the difference between an alert being sent and a person seeing it
  • Comfort, easy-to-use controls and clear messages

We are designing for women’s everyday safety, including those affected by gender-based violence. We still need to listen to users and test the devices. Whether you wear one is always your choice. You deserve support either way.

A First Look

One family. People you trust.

Our plan is simple: press for help, share what is available and keep your chosen people informed. Try the demo to explore the app with made-up details and practice alerts.

01 · Press for help

  • We are exploring an SOS button you can find by touch
  • An alert should start with a clear choice to ask for help
  • A sign that the button was pressed must not mean an alert was delivered

02 · Share where you are

  • Show when your location was recorded
  • Make old or uncertain locations clear
  • Say clearly when your location is not available

03 · Choose your people

  • Invite someone you trust and let them accept
  • Choose who can see a particular alert
  • Stop future sharing when you choose

04 · Know what happened

  • An alert being recorded does not mean help is on the way
  • See when a chosen person says they have seen it
  • Your alert should not close just because you stop moving

This is a demo, not a live safety service. Practice alerts do not send texts, phone notifications or messages to emergency services. The devices still need tests for mobile signal, charging, comfort and the SOS button.

Where we started

Mzansi Connect began with a tracker for livestock. That work helped us learn, but a device for an animal is not ready to be worn by a person. Our personal-safety designs need their own fit, comfort, privacy and safety tests.

What’s Next

Build with care. Prove each step.

We still have ideas for useful technology across our communities. First, we want to understand what women need and test the basics of these three designs.

01

Long-Term Vision

Useful technology. More choice.

We want to build useful South African technology with the people who will use it. Women’s safety, privacy and freedom to choose come first.

“Technology built by Africans for Africans.”

We want products that are easy to understand, easier to look after and built around your choices. A personal-safety device can be one part of a plan for getting help. It cannot prevent every crime, promise rescue or replace emergency services.
